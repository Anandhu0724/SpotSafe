What is a
black spot?

Community reports help identify places that need attention. They are not police FIR data and do not make a spot official.

500 m

One road stretch

Reports are grouped when they fall within 500 metres of one another.

3 years

MoRTH threshold

On a National Highway, five fatal or grievous crashes, or ten deaths, meets the MoRTH screening criterion.

IRC:131

Priority for review

IRC AATC reaction orders compare annual crashes against road-type thresholds. Only an authorised review can confirm official status.

Community-flagged cluster means 2+ nearby reports. Potential black spot means reports meet a screening threshold. Verified black spot means an administrator has checked official crash data.
Practical ideas

Countermeasure
guide.

Examples informed by IRC:131 Section 7.4. Final engineering choices require a qualified road-safety assessment.

Single vehicle / run-off road

Likely causes: speed, sharp curves, poor visibility. Short-term: chevrons, delineators and rumble strips. Long-term: improve alignment and provide a crash barrier.

Pedestrian crashes

Likely causes: missing footpaths or unsafe crossings. Short-term: markings, signs and lighting. Long-term: raised crossings, refuge islands or pedestrian underpasses.

Same-direction crashes

Likely causes: unsafe overtaking, speed differences or poor lane definition. Short-term: restore lane markings and add reflective studs. Long-term: access and corridor improvements.

Junction crashes

Likely causes: conflict points and restricted sight distance. Short-term: signs and remove obstructions. Long-term: splitter islands, junction realignment or grade separation.

Head-on crashes on undivided roads

Likely causes: unsafe overtaking and lack of separation. Short-term: centreline markings and warning signs. Long-term: median or crash barrier where appropriate.

Railway crossing crashes

Likely causes: poor visibility, missing controls or unsafe approaches. Short-term: warning signs, markings and lighting. Long-term: grade-separated crossing where justified.
